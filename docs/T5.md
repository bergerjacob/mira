# T5 tasks and task variants (2026-11-02, Day 2 phase A3)

Two deliverables:

* `env/variants.py`: a seeded rotate / mirror / translate copy of any task, checked on every T0-T5 task.
* `env/tasks/t5/`: four new tasks, all accepted by the env gate and cross-checked in vanilla 26.2 with one JVM per capture. They are state machines, feedback registers and a fan-out-heavy multiplier, so they are less like the library than T3/T4. Their single source is `env/tasks/author_t5.py`.

Tests: `tests/env/test_variants.py` (83) and `tests/env/test_t5.py` (36), 119 in total, all offline.

## 1. Variants (`env/variants.py`)

* **Seed to element.** `seed % 8` picks one of the 8 elements: rot 0/90/180/270 (clockwise from above, the builder's convention) times mirror none/x. Seed 0 is the identity with no offset. Every other seed also translates the bbox min corner by a seeded offset (x and z in -40..40, y in 0..6).
* **What is transformed.** In the contract: bbox, build_box, fixed boxes and their states, ports (pos, facing, region box, `block_prop` property names and values), keep_clear, no_write and change_boxes. Outside it: the reference solution, and the stored broken variants (edit positions and states). Direction words and coordinate groups (`(0..1, 2..3, z=3)`, `[x, y, z]`) in the description, hints and port docs are rewritten too.
* **What is unchanged.** Vectors, sequences, depends, budgets, the eval seed and the reference stats, so R4 is unchanged. A variant is the same task moved rigidly.
* **Block states** go through the builder's tables via `simulation.blockstates.transform_state`, used read-only. These cover facing, axis, rotation, per-side wire properties, rail and stair shapes, hinges and chest types.
* **Tested properties:**
  * group laws on whole tasks (4 quarter turns are the identity for every task; a mirror applied twice is the identity);
  * every variant of every task validates, and the reference stays inside the build box and off the fixed cells;
  * broken edits commute with the transform;
  * translation alone gives identical per-output scores;
  * the door's hallway rules still apply after a move;
  * simulator passes on rotation-invariant tasks.

28 of 42 tasks are invariant in all 8 elements. Rejections cluster in sequential tasks (clock, flip-flops, counters, LFSR, lock) and in parts that use comparators.

**Main finding: these rejections come from the env's placement path, not from the circuits.**

* I replayed 12 rejected variants in vanilla 26.2, one JVM per capture (`variants_report.json` → `vanilla`): t1_clock, t1_t_flipflop ×2, t1_xor, t3_counter2 ×2, t3_counter4, t3_parity4, t3_adder8, t5_lfsr4 ×2, t5_lock4. The identity of t5_lfsr4 was replayed as a control. **Every one passes in vanilla.** The oracle tool's own Nucleation replay also passes every one; it loads the whole schematic with `TickSettleMode.Placement`.
* Translation alone never changed a verdict. For example, `t5_lfsr4` and `t3_counter2` with offset only pass; rot90 / rot180 at offset 0 fail.
* What differs is `env/runner.py`: it places blocks one by one through the engine (`{simulate=true}`, sorted by (y, x, z)), and the paste check compares that settled world with a Placement re-load. Rotating a circuit changes the order in which its parts are placed and updated. For sequential circuits this picks a different initial state, or a wire shape that differs only in its connections. `t3_adder8` rotated takes longer to settle each vector and runs past the 20000-tick total budget.
* `t3_adder8` rot90 is vanilla-pass but not tick-identical, which is its known transient divergence (docs/LIBRARY.md).

Consequences:

1. Today a rotated task can be **unsolvable in the env even with the correct circuit**. For training, randomise only over `allowed_variants(task_id)` (translation is always safe), or fix the runner (Request 1).
2. The env verdict depends on orientation for the same build, so an agent's pass and fail on two orientations of one design is partly placement order. The vanilla oracle is the referee.

## 2. T5 tasks

Every reference is a builder program over lib/ parts plus `wire_path` with box / avoid / floor_exists / check_spacing, the same conventions as T4 (devices are fixed scenery). The simulation/router.py module layer was **not** used: `python simulation/modules.py alu4` failed env checks when tried (the m*_Y → m2 nets delivered 0) and took 8 min.

* **Gate** = `env/validate_tasks.evaluate`: spec valid and non-vacuous, reference reward 1.0 with every invariant, the empty build fails, ≥ 3 broken variants fail. Five are stored per task.
* **Kill** = auto-mutants killed out of 24.
* **Vanilla** = `author_t5.py --oracle`, one JVM per capture (`env/tasks/t5/oracle_report.json`).

**Hidden checks are held out, not reordered.**

* Combinational: `comb_rows()` publishes a seeded subset of rows, enough to show both values of every output. The other rows of the table are hidden and never published, plus a seeded random stream in which several inputs change at once.
* Sequential: the hidden sequences have different event lists from the public ones and are longer, with random gaps and hold times. The python models in `author_t5.py` generate the expectations (`lfsr_xnor`, `lock4`, `mul2x2`, `seq_detector`, registered with `env.models.model` when the authoring module is imported).
* The stored JSONs need no model at load time; they carry explicit rows and sequences.
* Tests check all of this: `test_hidden_checks_are_held_out_not_the_public_ones_reordered` and `test_sequence_expectations_follow_the_python_model`.

### Difficulty notes

* **`t5_lock4`.** An FSM with a one-hot shift chain (4 `d_flipflop`s, 3 `and2` gates). Each press is ORed into a clock, and each stage's D = previous stage AND the right button.
  * The timing trap: the clock must reach every stage while that stage's D is still valid. The press lasts 20 ticks, and the clock bus runs west with ~10 ticks of skew.
  * The reference needed an 8-tick delay on the merged clock and an 8-tick delay on stage 0's D input. Without them the late stage-0 edge missed its own button.
  * The R taps cross the L bus through 2 crossovers. The lamp row and two buttons are fixed.
* **`t5_lfsr4`.** A shift register with an XNOR feedback path routed back against the chain.
  * Tapping Q3 at its lamp-stub corner gave an unpowered corner dust (a corner dust powers only what its two connections point at). The reference taps at the Q port.
  * Starting from all-off with XNOR feedback avoids the lock-up state.
* **`t5_mul2x2`.** 4 AND, 1 XOR, 1 NOT, 2 more AND.
  * The fixed layout forces a topological trick: each lever feeds one gate on its north side and one on its south side, since a floor lever powers the dust on every side.
  * A0 and B1 wrap round the south gate. The P1 lamp sits **inside** that loop (a floating lamp at y=2), so the XOR output never crosses an input line.
  * P2 = A1B1 AND NOT P0 and P3 = A1B1 AND P0 reuse P0.
* **`t5_seq_detector`.** A 4-place shift register (clock bus against the data direction) plus a NOR over the places that differ from the pattern: inverters where the bit must be 1, a repeater diode where it must be 0. The OR line has repeaters flowing towards the output inverter only.

## 5. Honest caveats

* Kill rates (17-19 of 24) are lower bounds. Survivors are repeater delay +1 and redundant wire → stone; the specs tolerate timing windows on purpose.
* Vanilla verdicts replay the spec's stimulus. A behaviour the spec never exercises is not checked: two buttons pressed in the same tick, presses < 70 ticks apart, DIN changing within 14 ticks of an edge.
* The `t5_lock4` spec says presses are ≥ 70 ticks apart. A faster player would need a different (faster) design, and the reference does not support it.
* Variant text rewriting covers direction words and numeric coordinate groups. Free-text phrases like "left" or "right of the lever" are not rewritten; no shipped task uses them.
* `variants_report.json` for T4 checked 3 stored broken variants per element; T0-T3 and T5 checked 5.
